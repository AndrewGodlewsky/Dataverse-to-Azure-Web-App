Run it yourself

Nine lessons from "what are the parts" to "the secret is deleted and I know why it broke". The order follows the deployment path, so each one unblocks the next real step rather than completing a taxonomy.

~5 hours total Sessions of 30–45 minutes Assumes no Azure or Dataverse background

The lessons

  • ~35 min · no accounts needed 01 — Five parts and one request The five components, the one-port rule, what a BFF is, and the seven hops a click takes. Everything else attaches here.
  • ~40 min · unblocks the IT request 02 — How the app proves it may read App registration, application user, security role — three objects in two portals, and why the registration needs no API permissions.
  • ~30 min · unblocks the viewer group 03 — How your colleagues get in Easy Auth's three switches, the one that defaults to your whole company, and the trade you made by reading at app level.
  • ~45 min · first step that costs money 04 — Making the Azure side exist Five commands, every flag explained, managed identity and RBAC, and what the meter starts charging for.
  • ~35 min · needs lesson 04 05 — Getting the image there, and running Cloud builds with no local Docker, the pull-on-restart trap, tag strategy, and reading the container log stream.
  • ~45 min · needs the credential 06 — Pointing it at real tables The mock-to-live switch, enough OData to read your own query, and the two marked edits — plus how to find what your columns are really called.
  • ~40 min · the defensible-decision lesson 07 — The limits that decide the architecture Three numbers, two of them already handled in your code, and the tripwire that would reopen the copy branch.
  • ~40 min · mock mode, nothing can break 08 — Changing what the dashboard shows The cross-filter loop in fifteen lines, ECharts as configuration, and why colour never gets hardcoded.
  • ~40 min · closes the mission 09 — When it breaks, and deleting the secret Bisecting the trace in four questions, what each status code names, and the upgrade that removes the last password.

These nine were written in one sitting

Normally each lesson is written after seeing how the last one landed, so it lands in your zone of proximal development. Written upfront, they are aimed at a predicted you. Tell your teacher when a lesson is too easy or too dense and it gets rewritten — the numbering stays, the content is not fixed.
